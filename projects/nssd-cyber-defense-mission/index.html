The Story: What Happened at NSSD?

North Star Software Developers had network servers compromised. Customer information and credit card numbers may have been exposed. The company needs a practical defense plan that both technical and non-technical stakeholders can understand.

🚨

The Problem

Some servers were compromised, and the company does not yet know whether the attack came from inside or outside the network.

🎯

The Goal

Protect customer information, payment data, software code, development systems, and everyday business operations.

🧭

The Plan

Use layered defenses: watch traffic, control access, detect attacks, find weaknesses, map the network, and keep good records.

Plain-English version

Think of NSSD like a city. The roads are network traffic, the buildings are servers, the locks are firewalls, the alarms are IDS/IPS tools, the inspections are vulnerability scans, the map is network assessment, and the security camera footage is log collection.

Meet the Security Layers

Click each card to expand it. Each layer explains what the tool does, why it matters, and what NSSD staff should do.

🚦

Traffic Analysis

Like watching the roads to see where cars are going.

Traffic analysis helps IT staff see which systems are communicating and whether anything looks unusual.

Why it matters: It can reveal scanning, suspicious connections, malware beaconing, and possible data exfiltration.

What to use: Wireshark, tcpdump, firewall logs, IDS alerts, and system logs.

What to do: Capture traffic, filter by protocol or IP address, inspect packet details, and save evidence securely.

🧱

Firewalls

Like security gates that decide who gets in or out.

Firewalls allow approved traffic and block traffic that is risky, unnecessary, or unauthorized.

Why it matters: A poorly configured firewall can let attackers into sensitive systems.

What to use: Perimeter firewalls, internal firewall rules, host-based firewalls, and firewall logs.

What to do: Use least privilege, document rules, enable logging, test rules, and use a default deny policy.

🚨

IDS/IPS

Like alarms and guards that detect suspicious behavior.

IDS tools alert staff when traffic looks suspicious. IPS tools may block that traffic automatically.

Why it matters: IDS/IPS tools can detect port scans, brute-force attempts, exploit attempts, and malware activity.

What to use: Network IDS sensors, host-based IDS agents, firewall-integrated IPS, and SIEM alerts.

What to do: Configure allowlists and blocklists, place sensors strategically, monitor alerts, and tune rules.

🔎

Vulnerability Assessment

Like a safety inspection before something breaks.

Vulnerability assessment finds weak spots before attackers can take advantage of them.

Why it matters: Missing patches, outdated software, weak encryption, and exposed services can lead to compromise.

What to use: Nmap, vulnerability scanners, asset discovery, and controlled penetration testing.

What to do: Define scope, scan ports and devices, review findings by severity, remediate, and rescan.

🗺️

Network Assessment

Like making a map of every road, building, and hidden shortcut.

Network assessment shows what devices exist, how they connect, and whether the network has risky paths.

Why it matters: NSSD cannot protect devices it does not know exist.

What to use: Network discovery, topology mapping, firewall logs, IDS/IPS alerts, and asset inventory.

What to do: Find rogue devices, detect suspicious connections, view topology, and correct weak segmentation.

📚

Auditing and Log Collection

Like keeping security camera footage and receipts.

Logs help NSSD prove what happened, when it happened, and which systems were involved.

Why it matters: Without logs, investigations become guesswork.

What to use: SIEM, firewall logs, IDS/IPS logs, authentication logs, endpoint logs, and vulnerability scanner reports.

What to do: Centralize logs, protect them, review alerts, correlate events, and retain records according to policy.

The Defense Flow

A simple way to understand the whole manual: find what exists, protect what matters, detect problems, respond fast, and improve after.

1. IdentifyKnow devices, data, systems, and weak spots.
2. ProtectUse firewalls, segmentation, patching, and access controls.
3. DetectWatch traffic, IDS/IPS alerts, endpoint logs, and SIEM events.
4. RespondValidate, prioritize, contain, investigate, remediate, and document.
5. ImproveUse lessons learned to update rules, tools, policies, and training.

Company-Approved Tools

These are the tools NSSD IT employees can use as part of a layered defense strategy.

Tool Category Plain-English Purpose Why NSSD Uses It
Wireshark / tcpdump Looks closely at network packets. Confirms what traffic actually moved across the network.
Firewalls Controls allowed and blocked traffic. Reduces unauthorized access and supports segmentation.
IDS/IPS Detects or blocks suspicious activity. Identifies attacks, scanning, exploit attempts, and policy violations.
Vulnerability Scanners Finds weak spots before attackers do. Supports patching, remediation, and risk prioritization.
Network Discovery / Topology Mapping Maps devices and connections. Finds rogue devices, unknown paths, and segmentation problems.
Endpoint Monitoring Watches individual computers and servers. Shows whether suspicious network activity affected a host.
SIEM / Centralized Log Collection Collects security records in one place. Connects related events and supports investigations, audits, and reports.

Cybersecurity Glossary

Quick definitions for non-technical readers.

PacketA small piece of data traveling across a network.
FirewallA security control that allows or blocks traffic based on rules.
IDSAn intrusion detection system that alerts when activity looks suspicious.
IPSAn intrusion prevention system that can block suspicious activity.
VulnerabilityA weakness that could be exploited by an attacker.
SIEMA system that collects and connects security logs from many tools.
Rogue DeviceAn unknown or unauthorized device connected to the network.
SegmentationDividing a network into smaller protected zones.
LogA record of what happened on a system, tool, or network device.
